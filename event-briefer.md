## I. Background

### A. Rationale

The HackForGov Capture-the-Flag (CTF) Competition is the Department of Information and Communications Technology's (DICT) flagship cybersecurity competition and one of the country's premier cybersecurity capacity-building initiatives. First launched in 2019, the competition was established to provide students with a practical platform to develop, enhance, and demonstrate cybersecurity skills through real-world, scenario-based challenges.

Following a temporary suspension during the COVID-19 pandemic, HackForGov successfully resumed in 2023 and has continued to grow as a national platform for identifying and nurturing emerging cybersecurity talents. The competition brings together students, academic institutions, cybersecurity professionals, industry partners, and government stakeholders to promote cybersecurity awareness, technical excellence, collaboration, and innovation.

HackForGov features various cybersecurity challenges covering domains such as digital forensics, cryptography, steganography, web security, network security, and other cybersecurity disciplines. Through these simulated environments, participants are provided opportunities to apply technical knowledge, analyze security scenarios, develop problem-solving skills, and experience practical approaches in addressing cyber threats.

Since its relaunch, HackForGov has demonstrated continuous growth in participation and national reach. In 2023, the competition recorded 576 participants organized into 144 teams during the regional qualifying rounds. Participation increased in 2024 with 784 participants comprising 196 teams, while 2025 recorded 852 students forming 213 teams nationwide. This growth reflects the increasing interest of Filipino students in cybersecurity and the importance of developing future cyber defenders.

Beyond being a competitive platform, HackForGov serves as a national cybersecurity talent identification and development initiative that strengthens collaboration among government, industry, and academic institutions. Through its hands-on Capture-the-Flag format, the competition develops participants' technical proficiency, problem-solving abilities, teamwork, and ethical responsibility in addressing cybersecurity challenges.

Recognizing the importance of developing globally competitive cybersecurity professionals, HackForGov also serves as a pathway for outstanding participants to be considered for international cybersecurity competitions and capacity-building initiatives. Previous HackForGov participants and winners have been considered for opportunities to represent the Philippines in regional cybersecurity competitions, including the ASEAN Cyber Shield (ACS) Hacking Contest and the ASEAN–Japan Cybersecurity Capacity Building Centre (AJCCBC) Cyber SEA Game.

The ASEAN Cyber Shield (ACS) Hacking Contest, implemented under the ASEAN Cyber Shield Project supported by the ASEAN-Korea Cooperation Fund and led by the Korea Internet & Security Agency (KISA), brings together cybersecurity talents from ASEAN Member States through Capture-the-Flag-based technical competitions designed to enhance regional cybersecurity capabilities. The competition provides participants opportunities to test their technical skills, engage with cybersecurity professionals, and strengthen regional cooperation in addressing emerging cyber threats.

Similarly, the AJCCBC Cyber SEA Game provides young cybersecurity talents from ASEAN Member States with opportunities to enhance their knowledge and skills through a regional Capture-the-Flag technical competition. Participation in the Cyber SEA Game requires official nomination by the designated focal point of each ASEAN Member State and compliance with the eligibility requirements established by AJCCBC.

Through HackForGov 2026, exceptional participants may be considered for nomination to represent the Philippines in international cybersecurity competitions, subject to applicable eligibility requirements, assessment, and official selection processes. These opportunities reinforce HackForGov's role in cultivating future cyber defenders capable of contributing to both national and regional cybersecurity resilience.

### B. Objectives

The primary objective of HackForGov 2026 is to cultivate cybersecurity-ready students equipped with the technical knowledge, critical thinking skills, and ethical responsibility necessary to contribute to the country's digital defense.

Anchored on the theme "Decoding Youth Innovation, Anchoring One Nation's Digital Defense," the competition aims to harness youth innovation and technical capability in strengthening national cybersecurity resilience.

Specifically, HackForGov 2026 aims to:

1. Enhance participants' practical cybersecurity skills through hands-on Capture-the-Flag challenges involving threat identification, analysis, investigation, and response;
2. Develop participants' ability to apply cybersecurity concepts in simulated real-world scenarios involving digital security incidents and vulnerabilities;
3. Encourage collaboration, innovation, teamwork, and ethical cybersecurity practices among students;
4. Identify and nurture emerging cybersecurity talents who may contribute to the country's cybersecurity workforce;
5. Provide a pathway for outstanding cybersecurity talents to be considered for international competitions and capacity-building initiatives, including the ASEAN Cyber Shield (ACS) Hacking Contest and the ASEAN–Japan Cybersecurity Capacity Building Centre (AJCCBC) Cyber SEA Game, subject to applicable eligibility requirements and official nomination processes.

## II. Target Venue and Participants

### A. Venue

The venues for the HackForGov 2026 Regional Qualifiers will be identified and finalized by the respective Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) Regional Offices based on accessibility, technical capability, and logistical requirements.

The National Capital Region (NCR) leg and National Finals will be conducted within Metro Manila to provide a centralized venue equipped with reliable infrastructure, connectivity, and accessibility for finalists, organizers, and stakeholders.

### B. Participants

HackForGov 2026 is open to all currently enrolled college, university, and senior high school students who are interested in cybersecurity and related fields.

Participants must be twenty-six (26) as of the competition year (must not turn 27 during the competition year; born on or after 1 January 2000).

Students pursuing master's or doctoral degrees are not eligible to participate to ensure that the competition remains focused on developing undergraduate and senior high school cybersecurity talents.

To maintain fairness and ensure a level playing field, students pursuing master's and doctoral degrees will not be eligible to participate. This decision aims to prioritize the engagement of undergraduate students and aligns with the competition's objective of developing the next generation of cybersecurity professionals.

Participants must comply with the competition rules, registration requirements, and other guidelines issued by DICT and NCERT.

## III. Joining Process

### A. Team Composition

Each participating team must consist of four (4) members.

Team members must belong to the same school or educational institution.

Teams may be composed of students from different academic programs, provided that all members are officially enrolled in the same school.

Each participant must agree to comply with the competition guidelines, rules, and ethical standards set by DICT and NCERT.

### B. Submission of Letter of Intent

Interested teams must submit a Letter of Intent signed by all team members and an authorized school representative, such as a department head, chairperson, adviser, or director.

The Letter of Intent shall confirm the team's interest in participating in HackForGov 2026 and acknowledge their commitment to comply with the competition requirements.

### C. Submission of Curriculum Vitae (CV)

Each team member must submit an updated Curriculum Vitae highlighting relevant academic background, cybersecurity-related skills, certifications, training, projects, competitions, and other related experiences.

Submitted CVs may be used as part of the participant profiling and talent identification activities of NCERT.

### D. Upload Documents

All required documents, including but not limited to the following, must be submitted through the official registration platform:

- Letter of Intent;
- Individual Curriculum Vitae of all team members;
- Proof of enrollment or other school-related requirements; and
- Other documents that may be required by NCERT.
- Incomplete submissions may not be considered for qualification.

### E. Waiver for Minor Participants

Participants below eighteen (18) years old must submit a signed parental or guardian consent waiver prior to participation.

The waiver shall confirm parental or guardian approval for the participant's attendance and participation in HackForGov 2026 activities.

### F. School Representation

Each school may submit a maximum of three (3) teams for participation, subject to the evaluation and approval of DICT and NCERT.

After submission and validation of all requirements, NCERT shall review the applications and release the official list of qualified teams and participants.

Qualified teams shall receive further instructions regarding competition schedules, technical orientation, and event guidelines.

## IV. Event Information

### A. Competition Mechanics

HackForGov 2026 shall follow a Capture-the-Flag (CTF) competition format wherein participants solve cybersecurity challenges designed to assess their technical knowledge, analytical capability, and problem-solving skills.

The competition shall include challenges covering cybersecurity domains such as:

- Open Source Intelligence (OSINT)
- Web Application Security;
- Network Forensic and Security; and
- Miscellaneous Cybersecurity activities.

Each challenge contains a hidden flag in the required format that participants must identify and submit through the designated CTF platform to earn points.

### B. Challenge Point System

Each cybersecurity challenge shall be assigned a difficulty level and corresponding point range. The difficulty classification shall be as follows:

| Point Range    | Difficulty Level |
| -------------- | ---------------- |
| 01–30 points   | Easy             |
| 31–70 points   | Moderate         |
| 71–100 points  | Hard             |

The points awarded for each challenge shall depend on its assigned difficulty level. Participants shall earn the corresponding points upon successfully identifying and submitting the correct flag through the designated CTF platform.

The total points accumulated by each team shall determine the team's ranking during the regional qualifying competition. Individual scores may likewise be used in determining eligible participants for the designated Wildcard Slots, subject to the applicable qualification requirements.

### C. Activity Requirements

For the competition proper:

- Each team member is required to bring one (1) laptop device.
- Participants must ensure that their devices are prepared with necessary cybersecurity tools and applications.
- The recommended minimum specifications are:
  - **Processor:** Minimum 4.5 GHz
  - **Memory:** Minimum 8 GB RAM
  - **Connectivity:** Internet-ready device with Wi-Fi capability and/or Ethernet connectivity

The competition shall be conducted through face-to-face activities at designated venues.

### D. Methodology

HackForGov 2026 shall utilize an interactive, scenario-based learning approach through Capture-the-Flag exercises.

Participants will analyze simulated cybersecurity incidents, investigate digital evidence, identify vulnerabilities, and develop solutions using their technical knowledge and teamwork skills.

The competition is designed to simulate real-world cybersecurity operations where participants are expected to:

- Analyze security-related scenarios;
- Identify indicators of compromise (IOCs);
- Perform investigation and evidence analysis;
- Apply appropriate cybersecurity techniques;
- Recommend mitigation measures; and
- Prepare appropriate incident-related documentation.

### E. Platform

The Capture-the-Flag platform provides a gamified cybersecurity environment where participants solve technical challenges based on real-world cybersecurity concepts.

Through the platform, participants are expected to demonstrate skills related to:

- Incident investigation;
- Digital evidence analysis;
- Vulnerability identification;
- Security controls improvement;
- Cyber threat analysis; and
- Technical reporting.

All competition activities shall be conducted only within the authorized environment provided by NCERT.

Participants are strictly prohibited from performing unauthorized activities outside the competition environment.

### F. Authorized Tools

Participants may use commonly available cybersecurity, programming, and analysis tools necessary to solve the challenges, provided that their use is limited to the authorized competition environment.

Authorized tools may include, but are not limited to:

- **Network & Connectivity:** Port scanners, packet capture and network analysis tools, protocol analyzers, and DNS/WHOIS utilities;
- **Reverse Engineering & Code Analysis:** Disassemblers, decompilers, debuggers, binary analysis, and memory analysis tools;
- **Cryptography & Data Transformation:** Encryption/decryption tools, hash utilities, encoding/decoding tools, key-generation utilities, and cipher-solving tools;
- **Web Security:** HTTP clients, web proxies, web security testing tools, and web server analysis utilities;
- **Forensics & File Analysis:** File identification, metadata analysis, steganography, file recovery, and memory forensics tools;
- **System & OS Interaction:** System tracing, process inspection, file system, and system monitoring utilities;
- **Programming & Scripting:** Python, Perl, Bash, and other scripting languages; compilers, interpreters, and custom scripts;
- **Text & Data Processing:** Text-processing, pattern-matching, string extraction, and log-analysis utilities;
- **Container, Virtualization & Emulation:** Container analysis tools, virtual machines, and emulators; and
- **Operating Systems:** Microsoft's Windows OS, Linux OS, and other specialized cybersecurity distributions and environments, including Kali Linux, Parrot OS.

Participants may use custom scripts and tools developed by their team, provided that these are used only within the authorized competition environment.

The list above is not exhaustive. Challenge-specific instructions may further specify, restrict, or prohibit certain tools or techniques.

The use of AI-driven software, unauthorized external systems, or tools intended to interfere with the competition infrastructure or other participants remains prohibited.

### G. Selection of Champions and Wildcards

Each regional qualifying competition shall produce one (1) Regional Champion, who shall represent the region in the HackForGov 2026 National Finals.

In addition to the Regional Champion, each region shall be allocated one (1) Wildcard Slot, which shall be awarded to the highest-ranking eligible individual participant who is not a member of the Regional Champion team and who meets the prescribed qualification requirements.

The National Capital Region (NCR) shall be exempt from the one (1) Wildcard Slot allocation and shall follow a separate wildcard arrangement as determined by the organizers.

The Wildcard Slots are intended to recognize outstanding individual performers who demonstrate exceptional technical skills during their respective regional qualifying competitions but whose teams did not qualify as Regional Champions.

The four (4) selected Wildcard participants shall be combined to form one (1) Wildcard Team that will compete in the HackForGov 2026 National Finals.

Wildcard selection shall be based on the participant's individual performance and ranking during the regional qualifying competition, subject to the following:

- The participant must have completed the regional qualifying competition and obtained an eligible individual score;
- The participant must not be a member of the Regional Champion team;
- The participant must have complied with all competition rules and must not have incurred any penalty or disqualification; and
- The participant must meet any additional qualification requirements prescribed by the organizers.

The final list of Regional Champions and Wildcard participants shall be validated and officially announced by the organizers after the completion of the regional qualifying competitions.

## V. Code of Conduct

All HackForGov 2026 participants, coaches, organizers, volunteers, and other individuals involved in the competition are expected to uphold the highest standards of professionalism, integrity, respect, and ethical conduct throughout the event.

### A. Ethics and Fair Play

Participants shall:

- Compete honestly and independently and shall not engage in cheating, unauthorized collaboration, answer sharing, flag sharing, or any activity intended to gain an unfair advantage;
- Use only authorized tools, resources, and competition environments as specified by the organizers;
- Respect the competition rules, instructions, scoring mechanisms, and decisions of the organizers and authorized competition personnel;
- Refrain from attempting to disrupt, damage, manipulate, or interfere with the competition platform, infrastructure, network, systems, or other participants' devices;
- Not impersonate another participant or provide false or misleading information in connection with the competition; and
- Immediately report suspected cheating, rule violations, or technical vulnerabilities affecting the fairness or integrity of the competition to the organizers.

### B. Respectful and Inclusive Conduct

- Treat fellow participants, organizers, coaches, volunteers, judges, technical personnel, and other stakeholders with professionalism and respect;
- Refrain from harassment, intimidation, bullying, discrimination, threats, or abusive behavior in any form;
- Not engage in offensive, degrading, or inappropriate verbal, written, visual, or online conduct;
- Respect personal boundaries and differences in background, experience, identity, and technical ability; and
- Follow the instructions of organizers and venue personnel concerning appropriate conduct and event safety.

Any form of harassment or misconduct may be subject to appropriate disciplinary action, including removal from the competition or disqualification.

### C. Data Handling and Privacy

Participants shall exercise appropriate care when handling information obtained during the competition.

Participants shall:

- Use competition data, files, credentials, evidence, and other information solely for the purposes of solving authorized challenges;
- Not copy, retain, disclose, publish, distribute, or use competition data outside the authorized competition environment unless expressly permitted by the organizers;
- Not attempt to access personal information, confidential information, systems, accounts, or data that are outside the scope of the assigned challenges;
- Not share credentials, access tokens, flags, challenge files, or other restricted competition information with unauthorized persons; and
- Immediately report any accidental exposure, unauthorized access, or suspected compromise of sensitive information to the organizers.

Participants shall comply with applicable data protection, privacy, and cybersecurity requirements and with additional data-handling instructions issued by the organizers.

### D. Responsible Disclosure and Authorized Testing

HackForGov 2026 provides simulated cybersecurity environments for educational and competitive purposes. Participants shall limit all testing, exploitation, scanning, and other cybersecurity activities to the systems and environments expressly authorized by the organizers.

Participants shall:

- Conduct security testing only against designated competition systems and challenges;
- Not use vulnerabilities, credentials, techniques, or access obtained during the competition to target systems outside the authorized environment;
- Not attempt to gain unauthorized access to the devices, accounts, networks, infrastructure, or services of other participants, organizers, or third parties;
- Report vulnerabilities or unintended security issues discovered within the authorized competition environment through the official reporting mechanism designated by the organizers;
- Not publicly disclose, exploit, or distribute details of a vulnerability before receiving clearance from the organizers; and
- Follow the instructions of the organizers regarding the handling, documentation, and disclosure of reported vulnerabilities.

### E. Violations and Disciplinary Action

Any violation of this Code of Conduct or the HackForGov 2026 competition rules may result in appropriate disciplinary action depending on the nature and severity of the violation. Possible sanctions include, but are not limited to:

- Warning or formal reprimand;
- Deduction or cancellation of points;
- Removal from a challenge or competition activity;
- Disqualification of the participant or team;
- Removal from the competition venue; and
- Ineligibility for advancement, recognition, or other opportunities associated with HackForGov 2026.

The organizers reserve the right to investigate reported violations and determine the appropriate action based on the available evidence, applicable competition rules, and circumstances of the incident.

All participants are expected to read, understand, and comply with this Code of Conduct.

## Annex — Frequently Asked Questions

**1. What is HackForGov?**

HackForGov is DICT's flagship Capture-the-Flag (CTF) cybersecurity competition designed to provide students with hands-on experience in cybersecurity through technical challenges and simulated cyber threat scenarios.

The competition aims to develop cybersecurity skills, encourage innovation, and identify future cybersecurity professionals.

**2. Is HackForGov exclusive only for cybersecurity students?**

No. Students from different academic programs may participate, provided that they meet the eligibility requirements.

Cybersecurity knowledge, technical skills, and willingness to learn are encouraged among all participants.

**3. How do I register for HackForGov 2026?**

Interested participants must coordinate with their respective DICT Regional Offices and follow the official registration procedures provided through DICT and NCERT channels.

Teams must submit the required documents within the prescribed registration period.

**4. Is there any registration fee for the event?**

No, participation in HackForGov is free of charge.

**5. Do we need a team to accomplish the activity?**

Yes. Each team must consist of four (4) members from the same school.

**6. Are participants allowed to share answers or collaborate with other teams during the competition?**

No. Participants are strictly prohibited from sharing challenge answers, flags, solutions, hints, or any information that may provide an unfair advantage to other teams.

Each team must independently solve the assigned challenges using their own skills and authorized resources. Any form of unauthorized collaboration, including receiving or providing answers to other participants, may result in penalties, including disqualification.

**7. How are the winners determined?**

Winners are determined based on the total points earned from successfully completing cybersecurity challenges.

Regional winners shall advance to the National Finals.

Outstanding individual performers may be considered for wildcard slots subject to qualification requirements.

**8. What are the perks for the winning team?**

The winning team shall receive:

- Opportunity to represent their region and school in the HackForGov 2026 National Finals;
- Plaque of Recognition for each member of the winning team;
- Certificate of Recognition for the winning school; and
- Opportunity to be considered for nomination as Philippine representatives in international cybersecurity competitions, including the AJCCBC Cyber SEA Game 2026, subject to eligibility requirements, official nomination procedures, and final selection.

**9. Can non-registered participants join during the event?**

No. Only officially registered and approved participants shall be allowed to participate.

**10. Can team members be replaced?**

Team member replacements may be allowed subject to approval, validation, and compliance with registration requirements.

**11. Will non-winning teams still have opportunities to advance?**

Yes. Although only top-performing teams advance through the regular qualification process, outstanding individual performers may be considered for wildcard opportunities subject to qualification requirements.

**12. What if I encounter technical issues during the competition?**

Technical support personnel shall be available during the competition to assist participants with technical concerns related to the official competition environment.

**13. What happens if the entire team arrives late on the day of the competition?**

Teams are expected to arrive at the designated venue and complete the registration/check-in process within the prescribed call time.

If the entire team fails to arrive within the allowed grace period or before the official start of the competition, the team may be disqualified from participating in the competition to ensure fairness and avoid disruption to the competition flow.

**14. What will happen if only one or some members of the team arrive late?**

If a team member arrives late but the team has at least three (3) present and registered members, the team may proceed with the competition.

However, if only two (2) or fewer members of the team are present at the start of the competition, the team shall be considered incomplete and shall be disqualified from participating.

**15. Are participants allowed to use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools during the competition?**

No. The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, including but not limited to AI-powered assistants, automated solution generators, or similar platforms, is strictly prohibited during the competition.

HackForGov is designed to assess participants' individual cybersecurity knowledge, analytical skills, problem-solving capabilities, and teamwork. Participants must solve challenges using their own skills, available competition resources, and authorized tools only.

Any participant or team found using AI tools to generate answers, solutions, flags, or challenge responses may be subject to penalties, including disqualification from the competition.

**16. Are participants allowed to search online for references or use external resources?**

Participants who violate HackForGov 2026 rules, including unauthorized assistance, use of prohibited tools, interference with other participants, or any activity that compromises the integrity of the competition, may be subject to penalties including deduction of points, removal from the competition, or disqualification.

These provisions are aligned with international CTF competition practices, where activities such as obtaining answers from others, providing answers to other participants, interfering with other competitors, and violating competition rules may result in disqualification.

**17. What will happen if an emergency occurs during the competition (e.g., earthquake, fire, medical emergency, or other unforeseen incidents)?**

The safety and well-being of all participants, organizers, and stakeholders shall be the priority during HackForGov 2026. In the event of an emergency, such as an earthquake, fire, medical incident, security concern, or any situation requiring immediate action, participants must immediately follow the instructions of the event organizers, venue management, and authorized personnel.

The competition may be temporarily suspended, paused, or rescheduled depending on the severity of the situation and the assessment of the organizers. Participants must prioritize personal safety and comply with established emergency procedures, including evacuation protocols, if applicable.

If the competition is temporarily suspended due to an emergency, the organizers shall provide further instructions regarding the continuation of the activity, adjustment of competition time, or other necessary arrangements.

Participants shall not resume competition activities until clearance has been provided by the organizers and appropriate authorities.

**18. What happens if a participant experiences a health-related emergency during the competition?**

Participants who experience health concerns or medical emergencies during the competition must immediately inform the event organizers or designated support personnel for appropriate assistance.

The organizers may facilitate access to available first-aid support or emergency medical services. Depending on the circumstances, the affected participant may temporarily pause participation or withdraw from the competition for safety reasons.

The team's standing in the competition shall be assessed based on the applicable competition rules and circumstances surrounding the incident.

**19. Can participants leave the venue during the competition?**

Participants are expected to remain within the designated competition area during the activity.

If a participant needs to temporarily leave due to personal, health, or emergency reasons, they must inform the event organizers or assigned personnel before leaving the competition area.
